# Glossary

Key terms you'll encounter throughout this course.

---

**Accessibility**
: The design of products, devices, services, or environments so they can be used by people with disabilities. In technology, this includes screen readers, keyboard navigation, and other accommodations.

**Affordance**
: The relationship between an object's properties and a user's capabilities that suggests how something can be used. A button affords pushing; a handle affords pulling.

**Algorithm**
: A step-by-step procedure or set of rules for solving a problem or accomplishing a task, especially by a computer.

**Artificial Intelligence (AI)**
: The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems. This includes learning, reasoning, and self-correction.

**Autonomous Systems**
: Systems capable of performing tasks with little or no human intervention, using sensors, processors, and software to perceive, decide, and act.

**Big Data**
: Datasets that are too large or complex for traditional data processing. Characterized by volume, velocity, variety, and veracity.

**Copyright**
: Legal protection for original creative works, giving creators exclusive rights to reproduce, distribute, and display their work.

**Data**
: Raw facts, figures, or observations that have not yet been processed or organized. Data becomes information when given context and meaning.

**Data Analytics**
: The process of examining datasets to draw conclusions and identify patterns.

**Data Science**
: An interdisciplinary field using scientific methods, algorithms, and systems to extract knowledge from structured and unstructured data.

**Data Visualization**
: The graphical representation of data and information using charts, graphs, maps, and other visual elements.

**Database**
: An organized collection of data stored and accessed electronically, allowing for efficient storage, retrieval, and management of information.

**Design Thinking**
: A non-linear, iterative process that teams use to understand users, challenge assumptions, redefine problems, and create innovative solutions.

**Digital Divide**
: The gap between those who have access to modern information and communication technology and those who do not.

**Fair Use**
: A legal doctrine that permits limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes such as criticism, commentary, news reporting, teaching, and research.

**Filter Bubble**
: A state of intellectual isolation resulting from personalized searches and feeds showing users only information that confirms their existing beliefs.

**HCI (Human-Computer Interaction)**
: A multidisciplinary field focused on the design of computer technology and the interaction between humans and computers.

**Infographic**
: A visual representation of information designed to present complex information quickly and clearly to a general audience.

**Information**
: Data that has been processed, organized, or structured to provide meaning and context. Information is useful for making decisions.

**Information Retrieval**
: The process of obtaining relevant information from a collection of resources. Search engines are common information retrieval systems.

**Informatics**
: The study of how information technologies are developed, applied, and utilized in society—the intersection of data, systems, and people.

**Intellectual Property**
: Creations of the mind—inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, and symbols—protected by patents, copyright, and trademarks.

**Machine Learning**
: A subset of AI that enables systems to learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed.

**Net Neutrality**
: The principle that internet service providers should treat all internet traffic equally, without discrimination based on source, destination, or content.

**Privacy**
: The right of individuals to control their personal information and how it is collected, used, and shared.

**Security**
: The protection of information and systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction.

**SEO (Search Engine Optimization)**
: The practice of improving a website to increase its visibility in search engine results.

**Signifier**
: A visual or auditory cue that communicates how something should be used. Distinct from affordance.

**SQL (Structured Query Language)**
: A programming language used to communicate with and manipulate relational databases.

**UI (User Interface)**
: The visual elements through which a user interacts with a computer system or application.

**UX (User Experience)**
: The overall experience a person has when using a product or system, including how easy, efficient, and pleasant it is to use.

**Value-Sensitive Design**
: An approach to technology design that accounts for human values throughout the design process.

**WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines)**
: International standards for making web content more accessible to people with disabilities.
