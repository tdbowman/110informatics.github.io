## The Four Assignments, Explained

### 1. Engagement & Professionalism (30%)

This is the "be a good colleague" grade. It means:

- Attend class sessions on time and prepared (let Dr. Bowman know ahead if you can't make it)
- Read the materials before class and bring them into discussion
- Participate in discussions and in-class activities (speaking up gets easier with practice, and you're never required to have a polished opinion)
- Plan on roughly 3 to 4 hours of preparation for each hour of class
- Ask for help early and often: in class, office hours, or the Canvas Inbox (put '110' in the subject line)

### 2. Personal Reflections (35%)

Ten times during the semester, you'll post a reflection (roughly 300 to 500 words) connecting your own thinking to ONE of that week's readings.

- Think of it as a professional blog post: include at least one image, embed links to what you reference, and write in your own voice
- Post by class time each Friday; we discuss everyone's reflections together in class
- Good starting questions: How comfortable am I with this topic? What's missing from the article? Is this reading more or less relevant now than when it was written?
- Each module's Reflection Prompt box in this book gives you a ready-made angle

### 3. AI Journal (15%)

Across the semester you'll keep a journal of your interactions with AI tools: chatbots, image generators, music and video tools, whatever you explore.

- **At least 6 entries** of about 500 words each (roughly one every two weeks, starting week 2)
- Each entry includes at least one AI-generated image and APA-style citations where appropriate
- Entries must be distinct from your weekly reflections (expanding on one is fine; copy-paste is not)
- At the end, you'll turn your journal into a **podcast using an AI tool** (NotebookLM or similar) and share it with the class
- Format is flexible: document, blog, or vlog

:::{tip} This assignment requires AI use
The course AI policy prohibits passing off AI work as your own, but the AI Journal is the opposite case: here, using and critiquing AI *is* the assignment. Disclose what you used and reflect honestly, including the failures. The most interesting entries are usually about what went wrong.
:::

### 4. Research Report & Presentation (20%)

A small but real research project on an informatics topic covered during the semester.

- **Start with a research question** that can't be answered by a single source (you'll post candidate questions mid-semester for feedback and approval)
- **Use at least 7 sources, at least 3 of them scholarly**; consider adding a small human-subjects method like a poll or interviews
- **Present for no more than 6 minutes** in the final week(s), in whatever medium fits your findings: slides, poster, visualization, or website
- Report your question(s), method(s), findings, and sources, whatever format you choose
- Read the rubric on Canvas early, and practice the presentation out loud

## A Suggested Weekly Routine

1. **Weekend / early week**: read the module page in this book and the assigned readings
2. **In class**: discuss, ask questions, work the activities
3. **By Friday's class**: post your reflection
4. **Every other week**: add an AI Journal entry while the experience is fresh
5. **From week 8 on**: put a little time into your research project each week; a 6-minute presentation is much easier to build in six weeks than in one
